The problem

Five logins, five slices, no total

Each provider is happy to show you its own corner of your finances. None of them is interested in the rest.

You are probably doing fine. There's a mortgage that gets paid, a pension that quietly takes its percentage each month, some savings, maybe an ISA. Nothing is on fire.

But if someone asked what you're worth today, or what the mortgage will have cost by the time it's finished, or what that pension might actually pay you a year — you'd have to go looking. Through five apps, two paper statements and a login you can't remember.

And even then you'd have a set of numbers, not an answer. Adding them up in your head is the easy part. Knowing whether the total is going anywhere useful is the bit no single provider can tell you.

You can't name your number

Net worth isn't vanity — it's the one figure that tells you whether the last five years moved you forward. Most people have never sat down and worked theirs out, because doing it means opening six accounts at once.

The biggest debt of your life, barely examined

You know the monthly payment. Do you know the total you'll have handed over by the end of the term, or what putting an extra £100 a month at it would save you in interest? That is arithmetic, and it's rarely done.

"Am I on track?" has no answer without a target

A pension statement gives you a pot. A pot only means something next to the age you want to stop, the income you'd want, and what you're putting in between now and then.

Nothing keeps a record

Every app shows you today. Next January there's nothing to compare against, so real progress stays invisible — and so does a year where you quietly went backwards.

The tabs below are designed to be filled in from left to right — each one builds on the ones before it. You don't have to be precise; rough numbers still give you a useful picture, and you can come back and change them any time.

  1. Start with the first four tabs. Mortgage, Retirement, Investments and Cash at hand are each a calculator in their own right — put in what you owe, what you're paying in, and what you expect to grow at.
  2. Net Worth adds them all up. It reads straight from those four tabs, so fill them in first. It then projects how your home equity, pension, investments and savings build up together over the years ahead.
  3. Goal Planner works backwards. Tell it the net worth you want and the age you want it by, and it tells you roughly what you'd need to put away each month across each of the four areas to get there.
  4. Tracker is where real life comes in. Every month or year, log what you've actually got. It plots your real numbers against the estimate from your other tabs, so you can see whether you're running ahead or behind.
  5. Two extras sit above the tabs. Pay Off Early works out what it would take to clear your mortgage sooner, and Goal Tracker lets you set and follow your own individual goals with a target and a date.

Every tool has a "Log this to my diary" button. Use it whenever a number changes and your diary at the bottom of this page builds up a record you can look back on — and download as a PDF report any time.
