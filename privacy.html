2. The short version

We hold your email, your name, and the figures you type in. That's the substance of it. The figures — your mortgage balance, pension pot, savings, goals — sit in a database where every row is locked to your account.

We never see your card. Payment happens on Stripe's own page. The card number, expiry and security code go to Stripe and never reach this site.

There is no analytics, no tracking and no advertising here. Nothing measures what you click. Nothing follows you to another site. We don't sell or share your data with anyone for marketing, and there is no mailing list.

There's no bank connection. Every figure in your account is one you typed in yourself. Nothing is fetched from your bank, and there's nothing to revoke.

4. Why we're allowed to — our lawful bases

UK GDPR requires a lawful basis for each use of your data. Ours are:

  • Performance of a contract — your account, your figures, your diary and the £5 payment. You asked for a service; this is the data it takes to give it to you.
  • Legitimate interests — keeping the site running and secure (server logs), and reading and replying to messages you send us. We think you'd expect both; if you disagree, see your rights.
  • Legal obligation — records of the payment itself, which have to be kept for tax and accounting purposes.

We don't rely on consent for any of the above, because there's nothing optional going on: there's no marketing, no profiling and no advertising to consent to.

5. Payments and card details

The £5 signup payment is taken by Stripe on Stripe's own hosted checkout page. When you press the pay button, your browser leaves this site and goes to Stripe.

This means your card number, expiry date and security code are typed into Stripe's page, are sent to Stripe, and never pass through this site's code, servers or database at any point. There is nothing here to steal: no card number, no partial card number, no expiry date.

What comes back to us afterwards is a confirmation that a payment succeeded, tied to a Stripe customer reference and a checkout session reference. Those are what let us mark your account as paid and, later, find your payment if you ask for a refund.

Stripe is a processor acting on our instructions for that payment, and also a controller in its own right for its fraud-prevention and regulatory obligations. What Stripe does with the data is set out in Stripe's own privacy policy at stripe.com/gb/privacy.

7. Google Fonts and other loaded files — the bit people leave out

This site is set in a typeface called Inter, which it loads from Google's font servers (fonts.googleapis.com and fonts.gstatic.com). The pages that talk to your account also load the Supabase browser library from jsDelivr, and the dashboard loads the library that builds your PDF report from Cloudflare's cdnjs. (This policy page and the Terms load neither — only the typeface.)

Being straight about what that means: your browser makes a direct request to Google's servers (and to those CDNs) every time you open a page here, and that request carries your IP address, along with your browser and operating system details and the address of the page you're viewing. We don't ask for that, we don't see it, and we get nothing back from it — but it happens because of a choice we made about how the site is built, so you deserve to know about it.

Google's handling of those requests is covered by the Google Privacy Policy. Google states that Fonts requests are not used to profile you or serve you adverts, and that it does not set cookies for them — but the IP disclosure is real regardless, and if you'd rather avoid it entirely, a content blocker that stops third-party fonts and scripts will do so. The site is built to stay readable without them.

8. Cookies and browser storage

Financial Diary sets no tracking cookies, no advertising cookies and no analytics of any kind. Nothing here counts your visits, records what you click, or follows you to other sites. There's no consent banner because there is nothing to consent to.

Two things are stored in your own browser, and both stay on your device:

  • Your sign-in token. When you log in, the Supabase client library saves your session in your browser's localStorage so you're not thrown out the moment you refresh the page. Signing out removes it. This is strictly necessary for the site to work at all.
  • One preference, fd-howto-open. It records whether you've collapsed the “How this works” panel on the dashboard. It holds the word true or false and nothing else. Clearing your browser data resets it; the site works fine without it.

Stripe's own checkout page — which is Stripe's site, not ours — does use cookies, for fraud prevention and to make the payment work. That's covered by Stripe's privacy policy.

9. How it's kept safe

Your data lives in a PostgreSQL database at Supabase with row-level security switched on for every table that holds anything of yours. In plain terms: each row is stamped with the account id that created it, and the database itself checks that stamp on every single read and write. A signed-in member asking for someone else's rows doesn't get an error message — they get nothing back, because the database will not hand the rows over in the first place. This is enforced in the database, not in the web page, so it can't be sidestepped by fiddling with the browser.

Passwords are stored by Supabase Auth as one-way hashes — they can't be read back, by us or by anyone with access to the database. Traffic to and from the site is encrypted in transit (HTTPS).

Messages sent through the contact form are handled slightly differently and deliberately so: anyone can write one, but the site has no permission to read any of them back. The only way to read contact messages is by signing in to the Supabase dashboard as the owner. That way a bug in the website can never turn into a list of everyone's email addresses.

No system is perfect, and we won't claim otherwise. If something does go wrong in a way that puts your rights at risk, we'll tell the ICO within 72 hours as the law requires, and we'll tell you if you're likely to be affected.

10. How long we keep it

  • Your account and everything in it — for as long as you have an account. Ask us to delete it and it goes, along with your diary, goals and check-ins.
  • Contact messages — kept while they're being dealt with and for a reasonable period afterwards in case you come back about the same thing, then deleted.
  • Payment records — the record of the £5 transaction is kept for six years after the end of the relevant accounting period, because UK tax law requires it. That record is the transaction, not your financial figures.
  • Server logs — held by Vercel and expired on Vercel's schedule, which is a matter of weeks rather than years.

12. Your rights

Under UK GDPR you have the following rights over your personal data. All of them are free to use, and we'll respond within one month.

  • Access — ask for a copy of what we hold about you.
  • Rectification — have anything wrong put right. Most of it you can simply correct yourself; your email address is the one we currently have to change for you.
  • Erasure — ask us to delete your account and everything in it. We'll do it, subject only to the payment record we're legally required to keep. Deletion is permanent: there's no backup copy of your diary to restore afterwards.
  • Portability — ask for your data in a structured, machine-readable file you can take elsewhere. You can also generate a PDF report of your figures yourself, from the dashboard, at any time.
  • Restriction — ask us to stop using your data while a dispute about it is sorted out.
  • Objection — object to any processing we do on the basis of legitimate interests.
  • Rights around automated decision-making — for completeness: there isn't any. Nothing here makes a decision about you. The tools do arithmetic on figures you typed in and show you the result.

To use any of these, message us through the contact form or email [YOUR CONTACT EMAIL] from the address on your account. If we can't confirm it's really you, we may have to ask a couple of questions about the account first — that check protects you, not us.
