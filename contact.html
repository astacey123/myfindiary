I've forgotten my password

You can sort this yourself. Go to the log-in screen and click “Forgot password?”, which sits just above the password box. Put in the email you signed up with and you'll be sent a link that opens a “Set a new password” screen back here on the site.

Three things worth knowing before you write in:

  • The email comes from our sign-in provider, Supabase, not from a friendly-looking address — so check your spam or junk folder.
  • The confirmation on screen says the same thing whether or not that address has an account. That's deliberate — it stops anyone using the form to find out who has signed up — so a quiet inbox doesn't necessarily mean something has gone wrong.
  • Reset emails are rate-limited to roughly a couple an hour. If you've already clicked it several times in quick succession, the later ones simply won't be sent. Wait an hour and try once, rather than trying again straight away.

Still nothing an hour later? Use the form and say which email address you're trying.

I paid but I can't get into my account

Here's what's meant to happen. You create the login first, then you're sent to Stripe's own checkout page to pay the £5. When Stripe confirms the payment it calls back here, your account is marked as paid, and the dashboard unlocks. That callback normally lands within a few seconds.

When you come back from Stripe the site waits and re-checks for about twelve seconds. If the callback is slower than that, you'll be left on the “Almost there” screen even though the money has gone. Don't pay again. Wait a minute, then reload the page or sign out and back in — most of the time it has caught up by then.

If it still won't unlock, write in with:

  • The email address you used at Stripe's checkout — Stripe lets you type a different one there, and if it doesn't match your account email that's usually the whole problem.
  • The email address on your Financial Diary account, if it's different.
  • Roughly when you paid — the date and the hour is plenty.

That's enough to find the payment in Stripe and unlock the account by hand.

I'd like a refund

Fair enough — it's a £5 one-off payment, not a subscription, so there's nothing to cancel and nothing that will bill you again either way.

Refunds are considered within [YOUR REFUND WINDOW — e.g. 14 days] of payment. Send the form below with the email you paid with, roughly when you paid, and a line about what didn't work for you — that last bit isn't a hoop to jump through, it's genuinely how the thing gets improved.

Refunds go back through Stripe to the card you paid with; there's no way to send it anywhere else. Once it's issued, banks typically take five to ten working days to show it. The full position is set out in the Terms of Use.

How do I change my email address?

There's no self-serve way to do this yet — the account doesn't have a settings screen, so it has to be done for you.

Write in from the address currently on the account (that's how we know it's you), and say what you'd like it changed to. Your diary entries, goals, check-ins and paid status all stay attached to the same account, so nothing is lost and you won't be asked to pay again.

If you've lost access to the old mailbox entirely, say so in the message. You'll be asked a couple of things only the account holder would know instead — roughly when you signed up, and the last few figures you logged.

Is my data private? Who can see my figures?

Your figures are stored in a Supabase (PostgreSQL) database with row-level security switched on for every table. Each row carries the account id that created it, and the database checks every single read and write against the account making the request. Another member asking for your rows doesn't get refused with an error — they get an empty result, because as far as the database is concerned your rows aren't there.

Card details never touch this site. Payment happens on Stripe's own hosted checkout page, so the card number, expiry and security code go to Stripe and nowhere near this site's database. What is stored here is a Stripe customer and session reference, which is just enough to match a payment to an account.

There's no bank connection to authorise, no analytics, and no tracking or advertising cookies. The Privacy Policy sets out the whole picture, including the bits people usually leave out.

Something's wrong with a calculation

Please do send these — they're the most useful messages that arrive. To be able to reproduce it, the message needs:

  • Which tab or page — Mortgage, Retirement, Investments, Cash at hand, Net Worth, Goal Planner, Tracker, Pay Off Early or Goal Tracker.
  • Every figure you typed in on that tab, including the slider positions and any growth or interest rate. Leaving one out usually means it can't be reproduced.
  • The number it showed you, and the number you expected — plus, if you can, how you worked yours out.

Two things that account for most reported differences, worth ruling out first: the projections compound the growth rates you set, so a different assumption gives a different answer without anything being broken; and the mortgage tools assume a capital repayment mortgage held at one fixed rate for the whole term, so they won't match a lender's quote that reverts to a variable rate after a fixed period.
